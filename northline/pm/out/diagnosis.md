# Diagnosis

## Headline

The nurses are the ceiling — and we are about to put 2.5× the weight on it

## What the board sees

The board deck reports 2,400 escalations a week and a median nurse response of about 30 hours, which looks manageable. Those numbers imply the system is handling a large, steady volume with reasonable turnaround. Nothing in the deck flags a staffing problem.

## What the logs show

The actual escalation rate from the queue logs is 155 a week — one-fifteenth of the board figure — and the real median response is 28.9 hours, close to what was reported, but the 90th-percentile response is 69.7 hours, meaning the worst-served tenth of patients wait nearly three days. There are 238 escalations that have gone unanswered beyond 24 hours right now, and 219 patients have gone silent after raising a concern, which is the clearest sign that people are falling through. Nearly four in ten messages landing in the nurse queue are not clinical at all — insurance questions, pharmacy logistics, device troubleshooting — tasks that consume nurse time without requiring a nurse.

## The bottleneck

Twenty-two nurses are the single constraint in this system. Every unresolved patient need, whether clinical or not, eventually reaches them, and there is no slack: the model projects that signing the Prairie contract will push median nurse response from 29 hours to 336 hours — fourteen days — with the same headcount serving 2.5 times the patients. The technology is not the limit; the people are.

## Why signing Prairie makes it worse

Adding 60,000 patients multiplies the escalation volume proportionally while the nursing team stays fixed at 22, so each nurse absorbs roughly 2.5× her current load overnight. The 39% non-clinical share of the queue does not shrink on its own, meaning a large fraction of that new flood will still be insurance calls and pharmacy questions consuming the same clinical hours. At a projected 336-hour median response, patients with urgent chronic-care needs would routinely wait two weeks for a nurse callback, which is a patient-safety exposure, not just a service-quality one.

## The one metric to watch

Median nurse response time, broken out by urgent and non-urgent. It is the single number that collapses staffing load, queue composition, and patient safety into one reading — and the model already shows it moving from 29 hours to 336 hours the moment the contract is signed.
